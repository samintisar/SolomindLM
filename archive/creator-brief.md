# SolomindLM — Creator Collaboration Brief

## What is SolomindLM?

SolomindLM is a free AI study and research notebook. Students upload lectures, PDFs, slides, or notes, then chat with those sources (answers come with citations) and turn them into flashcards, quizzes, and written practice questions they can edit. Researchers can do the same with papers: discover or import a reading list, chat across it, and run literature review with formatted citations (APA, MLA, Chicago, and more). ChatGPT will invent an answer from the internet. SolomindLM answers from the files you uploaded.

## The core use case to show

One demo, shot as a real session — not a feature tour. Pick the angle that matches **your** audience. Don’t try to show study tools and literature review in the same 20–60 seconds. We can seed a notebook for you either way.

**Study (default for most creators):** Upload (or open) a lecture/PDF → ask a question and show the cited answer, **or** generate flashcards/a quiz from that file so the cards appear on screen.

Optional framing: before vs after — scrolling the PDF / making Quizlet cards by hand vs getting cards or a cited answer in seconds.

**Research (if your audience is grad students / academics):** Open a small reading list of papers → ask a question across them, **or** run literature review so a synthesis with citations appears on screen. Import (DOI / reference manager) is worth a quick flash if it’s natural; don’t turn the video into a how-to on file formats.

Optional framing: before vs after — skimming ten PDFs vs getting a sourced synthesis you can check.

Please show the product UI on screen for a few seconds. A talking-head review with no app doesn’t prove what it is.

## Key points to hit (pick 2–3, don't force all of them in)

- It works from *your* materials (lectures or papers), not generic AI knowledge
- Answers include citations, so you can check the source instead of trusting the model blindly
- Study angle: it doesn’t just chat — it turns those sources into flashcards, quizzes, and written questions
- Research angle: literature review and paper chat with formatted citations — you still verify; it doesn’t replace a systematic review

## Tone & style

- **Full creative freedom** — you know your audience and format better than we do
